1. The Tech Campus Analogy

Think of a VPC (10.0.0.0/16) as an entire gated corporate campus with 65,536 total room numbers (IPs).

Subnets (/24) are individual buildings on that campus with 256 rooms each:

  • Public Subnet: The campus lobby where external visitors (internet traffic) enter through the Application Load Balancer.
  • Private Subnet: Secure employee office floors (EC2 / EKS worker nodes) with no direct internet door.
  • Database Subnet: An underground high-security bank vault with zero internet access, reachable only from the private office floors.
2. Understanding The Slash (/16 vs /24)

Every IPv4 address has exactly 32 binary bits. The slash number (prefix) tells you how many bits are locked for the network name:

10.0.0.0/16: 16 bits locked
Remaining host bits = 32 - 16 = 16
Capacity: 2¹⁶ = 65,536 Total IPs
10.0.1.0/24: 24 bits locked
Remaining host bits = 32 - 24 = 8
Capacity: 2⁸ = 256 Total IPs

Rule of Thumb: Smaller slash number = Huge network (many IPs). Larger slash number = Smaller subnet (fewer IPs).

3. Why Always 2 Availability Zones?

In AWS, an Availability Zone (AZ) is an isolated physical data center.

A single subnet cannot span multiple AZs. Therefore, for High Availability (HA) and Disaster Recovery:

• AZ-1a: 1 Public, 1 Private, 1 DB Subnet
• AZ-1b: 1 Public, 1 Private, 1 DB Subnet
Total: 6 Subnets (The Standard 3-Tier Architecture)

If a physical power failure hits AZ-1a, AWS immediately reroutes traffic to AZ-1b with zero customer downtime!
